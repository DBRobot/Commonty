# Terms of use

Commonty is run by David Bascom, together with the members who host its
boxes. These terms are between you and David. By using Commonty you
agree to them.

They cover three kinds of people:

- **Members**, who have an account and use Commonty's services.
- **Guests**, invited by a member to play on a game server, with access to
  games and nothing else.
- **Visitors** to the public site and the demo.

## What Commonty is

Commonty is run by its members, for its members, on computers they own.
It is not a company, and nobody pays to use it.

**Who can be a member.** For now, membership is open to:

- people who contribute hardware: a computer that adds real storage or
  computing power to the network. A device that adds next to nothing, a
  Raspberry Pi for example, does not count;
- people David invites himself.

**Guests** are free too. A guest is invited by a member through a game
link, and can use games and nothing else.

Everyone who uses Commonty understands what that means. It is not a big
corporation with paid staff and a contract to be perfect. It is people
looking after something together, as well as they can. Keep your own
copies of anything you could not bear to lose.

## Age

You must be at least 13 to use Commonty. Commonty is not meant for
children under 13 and does not knowingly collect anything from them; if
we learn that an account belongs to someone under 13, it is closed and its
data is deleted. If you are under 18, you need a parent's or guardian's
permission, and they are responsible for how you use it.

## Hosting a box

If you host a box for Commonty, you agree to:

- keep it running and up to date;
- never tamper with it, or with the software Commonty runs on it;
- never try to read or recover anything it holds.

Everything a box holds for other members is locked, so a box that breaks
these rules exposes nothing. It is removed from the network.

## What is not allowed

You may not use Commonty to:

- store or share anything illegal, and never anything that sexually
  exploits children;
- store or share films, music or other work you have no right to copy;
- harass, threaten or abuse anyone, members and guests alike;
- attack the network, or try to read anyone else's data. Looking for
  security problems in good faith is welcome, under the
  security policy;
- send spam, mine cryptocurrency, or sell or give away access.

## When something goes wrong

Commonty cannot see what you keep locked, so it never scans your data.
Action only ever starts from a report by someone else.

David decides what happens. Anyone accused is told what was reported and
given a chance to show they are using Commonty properly before anything
is done. If someone is removed, their access ends at once, and their data is
deleted after the waiting period, as described in the privacy policy.

You can leave whenever you like.

before it takes effect.
